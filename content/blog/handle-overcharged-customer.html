Most shoppers who notice a pricing error will say something. In a fall 2025 University at Buffalo survey of 2,011 U.S. shoppers, 91% said they're likely to point out a pricing error to someone in the store. How that conversation goes decides whether they come back.

1. Believe the receipt

If the receipt shows the same item twice, or a price above the shelf tag, fix it. Don't send the customer to the aisle to prove it unless you have to. The time they spend arguing over a few dollars costs you more than the refund.

2. Refund it right away

Void the duplicate or refund the difference to the original payment method while they wait. A fix at the counter takes a minute. A shopper who has to call back later, or dispute the charge with their bank, leaves with a much worse impression.

3. Thank them

A customer who catches an error is helping you find a problem in your pricing. Say thanks, and mean it.

4. Tell them your policy

About a third of shoppers in the same survey didn't know whether their store had a fair way to make up for pricing errors. If you have a policy, post it at the registers and the service desk. Some retailers go further. Under Canada's voluntary Scanner Price Accuracy Code, for example, participating stores give the item free when it scans above the shelf price, up to a set limit.

5. Fix the cause

Log each error with the item and the reason. Expired sale tag, missed price change, double scan? Patterns show up quickly. The same survey found that shoppers who see errors frequently lose trust much faster, so repeat mistakes are the ones to stop first. 7 ways to reduce checkout pricing errors.

6. Make catching mistakes easy

The best moment to find an error is before the customer leaves the store. Encourage shoppers to check their receipts, and keep the service desk easy to find. See how Dupe Detective helps shoppers catch double scans in seconds.
