Pending charge showing twice? How to tell if it's a real duplicate

October 2, 2026

Short answer

If one of the two charges is still pending, it's usually an authorization hold, not a second payment. Debit card holds typically drop off within 1 to 5 business days. Credit card holds can last longer, and hotels and car rentals may hold funds for weeks. If both charges have posted, it's a real duplicate and you should contact the merchant.

Pending vs. posted

A pending charge is a hold. The merchant has asked your bank to set money aside, but the sale isn't final. A posted charge is the final amount, actually taken from your account or added to your balance.

Many merchants place a hold first and post the final charge later. Until the hold is released, the same purchase can show up twice.

Where this happens most

  • Restaurants and bars: the hold is for the bill before the tip. The final charge includes the tip, so the two amounts differ.
  • Gas stations: pay-at-the-pump holds can be larger than what you pumped, because the station doesn't know the total in advance.
  • Hotels and car rentals: holds for incidentals or deposits can last until after checkout, sometimes for weeks.
  • Online orders: some stores hold the amount when you order and charge it when the item ships.

How long holds take to drop off

Most pending charges settle or disappear within a week. Debit card holds commonly fall off within 1 to 5 business days. Credit card holds vary by bank and can take longer. Your bank's app or website often shows when a hold expires.

When it is a real duplicate

  • Both charges show as posted, not pending.
  • The amounts are identical and a few minutes apart.
  • A hold is still there well past the usual window for that type of merchant.

In any of these cases, contact the merchant first. If that doesn't fix it, dispute the charge with your card issuer.
