How to reduce pricing errors at checkout: 7 fixes for grocery stores

October 2, 2026

Short answer

Most checkout pricing errors come from shelf tags that don't match the register: sale tags left up after a promotion ends, price changes that reach the system but not the shelf, and items scanned twice. Audit tags on a schedule, remove expired sale tags the day a promotion ends, run your own price checks the way inspectors do, and make it easy for shoppers to catch mistakes before they leave.

1. Pull expired sale tags the day the promotion ends

When Consumer Reports sent shoppers to 26 Kroger-owned stores in 2025, they found more than 150 items with expired sale tags still on the shelf. On those items, the register charged an average of $1.70, or 18.4%, more than the tag showed. Make tag removal part of the promotion schedule, not an afterthought.

2. Audit shelf tags against the system weekly

Pick a sample of items each week, scan them at a register, and compare the result with the shelf tag. Focus on the categories where prices change most, such as produce, meat and anything on promotion.

3. Check yourself the way inspectors do

State weights and measures inspectors follow the price verification procedure in NIST Handbook 130. To pass, at least 98% of the checked items must ring up at the advertised price. Running the same kind of check internally tells you where you stand before an inspector does. What happens if a store fails an inspection.

4. Look at digital shelf labels

Digital shelf labels update from the same system as the register, which removes the gap between a price change and the paper tag. Walmart has said its digital labels make it easier to keep shelf prices aligned with checkout.

5. Train for the double scan

Many duplicate charges come from a scanner that doesn't beep, so the cashier scans again and both scans count. Multi-packs are another trap, when the pack and an item inside it both get scanned. Train cashiers to check the screen, not just listen for the beep, and to watch the item count.

6. Watch self-checkout

At self-checkout, shoppers double-scan items too, often when the bagging area acts up. Make sure attendants know how to void a line quickly, and put clear signs at the station telling shoppers to check the screen before paying.

7. Help shoppers catch what slips through

No process catches everything. A mistake the shopper notices at the register takes a minute to fix. The same mistake found at home costs you a phone call, a return trip or a card dispute, and some of the customer's trust. Encourage shoppers to check receipts before they leave, and make the customer service desk an easy place to fix it.
