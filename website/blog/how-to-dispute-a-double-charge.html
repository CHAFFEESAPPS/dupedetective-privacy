How to dispute a double charge with your bank or card issuer

October 2, 2026

Short answer

Ask the merchant to refund the duplicate first. If they won't, file a billing-error dispute with your card issuer. For US credit cards, your dispute must reach the issuer within 60 days after the statement showing the charge was sent. The issuer must acknowledge it within 30 days and resolve it within two billing cycles, and no more than 90 days.

Step 1: Make sure it's a real duplicate

If one of the charges is still pending, wait a few days. It's probably a hold that will drop off. How to tell a hold from a duplicate.

Step 2: Ask the merchant

Merchants can reverse a duplicate payment directly, and it's usually faster than a dispute. Have the date, amount and receipt ready. Write down who you spoke with and what they promised.

Step 3: Dispute it with your card issuer

If the merchant doesn't fix it, contact the bank that issued your card. Most issuers let you start a dispute in their app or on their website, which works for most people.

For credit cards in the US, the Fair Credit Billing Act gives you extra protection when you dispute in writing. Send a letter to the address your issuer lists for billing errors or billing inquiries. That is often a different address from the one for payments.

What to include

  • Your name and account number
  • The date and amount of the duplicate charge
  • A short explanation: "I was charged twice for one purchase."
  • Copies (not originals) of your receipt and statement

Sample dispute letter

I am writing to dispute a billing error on my account ending in [last 4 digits]. On [date], [merchant] charged me $[amount] twice for a single purchase. I am disputing the second charge of $[amount]. Copies of my receipt and statement are enclosed. Please remove the duplicate charge and any related fees.

Step 4: Know the deadlines

  • Credit cards: your written dispute must reach the issuer within 60 days after the statement with the error was sent. The issuer must acknowledge it within 30 days and resolve it within two billing cycles (no more than 90 days). You can withhold payment on the disputed amount while it's investigated, but keep paying the rest of your bill.
  • Debit cards: report the error within 60 days after the statement was sent. Banks generally have 10 business days to investigate, or longer if they give you a temporary credit. More on debit card timelines.

If your dispute is denied

Ask the issuer to explain the decision in writing. If you still think it's wrong, you can file a complaint with the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau at consumerfinance.gov/complaint.

This guide covers US rules and isn't legal advice. Check your card agreement for details.
