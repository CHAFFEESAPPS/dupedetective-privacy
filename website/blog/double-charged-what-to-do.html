Double charged? What to do next

October 2, 2026

You found an item rung up twice, or the same payment on your statement twice. Here's how to get the money back, starting with the quickest option.

1. Make sure it's a real duplicate

If one of the two charges is still marked pending, wait a few days. Temporary holds from restaurants, gas stations and hotels often drop off on their own. If you bought two of an item, both lines are correct.

2. Ask the store

Go back with the receipt, or call. Most stores refund a double-scanned item on the spot. For a payment that went through twice, ask the manager to reverse one transaction. Write down the date, who you spoke with and what they agreed to.

3. Dispute it with your card issuer

If the store won't fix it, contact your bank or card issuer and say you were charged twice. Have the receipt and the transaction dates ready.

  • Credit cards (US): Under the Fair Credit Billing Act, your written dispute must reach the issuer within 60 days after the statement showing the error was sent.
  • Debit cards (US): Under the Electronic Fund Transfer Act, report the error within 60 days after the statement was sent.

Don't wait for the deadline. Many issuers let you start a dispute in their app, and acting early makes the process faster. This guide covers US rules and isn't legal advice. Check your card agreement for details.

4. Keep the paperwork

Keep the receipt and any notes until the refund shows up on your statement. A scanned copy in Dupe Detective works if the paper fades.
