How to spot a duplicate charge on a receipt

October 2, 2026

Most duplicate charges aren't fraud. A cashier scans an item, the scanner doesn't beep, and they scan it again. Both scans register. Or a card reader hangs and the payment goes through twice. These mistakes are small, so they rarely get noticed.

Check the receipt line by line

  • The same item and price on two lines. Repeats are easy to spot when they sit next to each other. They're harder to see when other items come between them.
  • An item count that's too high. Many receipts print an item count near the total. Compare it with what's actually in your bags.
  • Weighed produce entered twice. Look for two weight lines (such as "1.32 LB @ 0.59/LB") for one bag.
  • A sale price that didn't apply. This isn't a duplicate, but it turns up during the same check. Look for the shelf price where you expected the discount.

Then check your card statement

Two charges for the same amount at the same store, minutes apart, usually means the payment went through twice.

A pending charge next to a posted charge is often not a duplicate. Restaurants, gas stations and hotels place a temporary hold, then post the final amount. The hold usually drops off within a few days. If both are still there after the pending period, treat it as a duplicate.

Make it a habit
